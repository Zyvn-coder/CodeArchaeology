# The second repository: review and freeze preparation

**What this is.** The independent review of
`docs/v0.5-second-repository-experiment.md` (the second repository's six
questions and their materials), asked for by the user before the pilot starts:
re-verify every claim the materials rest on, judge whether they meet the startup
conditions, and record what must be true before session 1.

**What this is not.** It is not a change to the frozen protocol, and it is not
the experiment. **Nothing here was measured on a person.** No participant was
recruited, no question was run, no second scorer was found or trained, no memory
was written. Where the materials were insufficient, this document supplies the
minimal specification — it does not claim the thing described has been done.

**Verdict, stated first and argued in §10: READY WITH DISCLOSED LIMITATIONS.**
No unresolved issue affects the fairness of the six questions or the
scoreability of a written answer (CONFIRMED by re-running every reachability and
calibration command). The Memory sub-experiment is **deferred by the user's
decision of 2026-10-09** (§7) and is annotated as such in the frozen session
script. The limitations that must appear in the experiment report are listed in
§10.

**How each claim is marked.** **CONFIRMED** = a command was run in this review
and its output is quoted or counted. **INFERRED** = reasoned from evidence that
exists but does not state it. **UNKNOWN** = the available evidence cannot settle
it — most often because it is a claim about participants, and there are none yet.
Every unresolved issue is tagged **BLOCKING** or **NON-BLOCKING** with the reason.

**What was re-verified, in one paragraph.** The project's HEAD, branch, test
suite and CI status; the identity of the frozen protocol (by hash); the tqdm
clone's HEAD and cleanliness; the tool's own view of that clone (event counts,
date ranges, blind spots, parse failures); all six questions' `grep` /
`git log --grep` / `git log -S` / `git blame` calibration commands; the six
`structure --history` reachability claims; the two conventions that bit the
derivation (commit vs author dates, rename-chain lifecycles); the four rejected
candidates' recorded measurements; the four-repository comparison table; and the
attrs baseline. The corrections this produced are applied to
`docs/v0.5-second-repository-experiment.md` and listed in §3.5.

*only* hit for the reason's distinctive words in the whole repository. T-2's
mechanism is stated in two docstrings, one of them embedded verbatim in
`README.rst:613`. Both have blame landing on the decision (T-1: 12 of 28 lines
`bee52fa1c`). A person who opens the file and reads it has the answer; a person
who greps for "flicker" or "overlap" has it in one command. So **the ceiling is a
property of the repository and the question, measured, not asserted.**
**INFERRED from that:** both arms should score 2 on T-1 and T-2, and a large
difference on them is more likely noise than signal. **UNKNOWN:** whether
participants actually reach the ceiling — no one has run these questions. A
participant who answers from the loop and stops reading (the plausible failure)
gives the pre-4.44.1 answer, which is T-1's most likely wrong answer and is
written into the anchors; that is the one way the ceiling could fail to hold, and
it fails in both arms.

**What the ceiling does not do.** It does not make the pair useless. The frozen
composition rule (§4.3) requires two L1s *precisely* so a tool that actively
misleads is detectable: §10's Harmful outcome fires when "the tool arm is worse
on the L1 questions". A pair that saturates in both arms is the expected,
pre-registered shape of an instrument control; a pair that saturates in the
control arm and *not* in the tool arm is the strongest possible finding. What the
pair cannot do is show a tool *benefit* — and §4.3 accepts that.

**Recommendation: keep both, unchanged.**
- They are the frozen instrument controls; deleting or replacing them would
  change the composition rule and the protocol, which this review may not do.
- Removing them would also remove the only test of the tool's most dangerous
  failure mode.
- What the *report* must do — not the questions — is pre-register the
  expectation: "T-1 and T-2 are expected to saturate; a result of both arms at 2
  is the control working, not a null result for the set." That sentence belongs
  in the limitations, and is listed in §10.

### 4.2 T-4: the negative control, kept on purpose

**What the control arm can get (CONFIRMED).** `git blame -L 228,239` → 12 of 12
lines `8ae4fc86` (2020-12-24); `git log -S "beta ** self.calls"` → `8ae4fc8`;
`git log --grep=EMA` → four commits including `e9bf3cd` ("better EMA accuracy
for early iterations", "fixes #1101") and `de4ae9f` (the 2018 function);
`git show 8ae4fc8` shows the class rewrite carrying the divisor. The tree says
nothing about the divisor (zero "bias" hits), so the tree cannot answer it — but
the message can, in one grep.

**What the treatment arm gets that the control does not.** Measured, and it is
*nothing that helps*: `structure --history` for `EMA.__call__` is a one-event
life at `8ae4fc86` — the same commit blame names — and the class life adds a
2026 formatting-era modification plus the fact that the pre-2020 function life
is not represented at all. The treatment arm's added artifacts do not include
`e9bf3cd` (the divisor's own commit); the control arm's `--grep=EMA` does.

**Could the added evidence change correctness, evidence quality or time?**
**INFERRED: no, in any direction that flatters the tool.** On correctness: both
arms have a one-command path to a commit that already contains the divisor, so
the *hard* half of the answer (what the divisor is for) is domain knowledge in
both arms; nothing the tool prints supplies it. On evidence quality: a 2 requires
naming a commit, and both arms can name `8ae4fc8`; the tool's list does not add a
second checkable artifact. On time: the tool's list is one command, like blame —
and unlike `--grep=EMA` it cannot even be run to reach `e9bf3cd`, so if the tool
saves time it saves it on a step the control also has. **This makes T-4 a
negative control in the strict sense: a question where the treatment's extra
information is empty, so a tool-arm improvement on T-4 would be evidence of
something other than the tool's information (familiarity, ordering, or noise).**

**Why it stays.** Two reasons, and the second is the one that matters. First,
the frozen composition needs two L2s and T-4 is a legitimate L2 by the
calibration tests (message-only reason, blame on the decision). Second, removing
it because it might not favour the tool is the one edit this review is explicitly
forbidden to make — "不能因为题目结果可能不利于工具，就将其排除". Keeping it is
also *informative*: if the tool arm beats the control on T-4, that result is
about participants, not about the instrument, and the report should say so.
**Recommendation: keep, and disclose in the report that T-3 and T-4 are both
blame-reachable questions where the tool's lifecycle view adds no informational
advantage — i.e. that the set's discrimination rests on T-5 and T-6.** That
sentence is part of the disclosure list in §10.

### 4.3 The disclosure neither ceiling nor control forces, but honesty does

Four of six questions (T-1, T-2, T-3, T-4) give the treatment arm no information
the control cannot get with one command; two (T-5, T-6) are the discriminating
pair. This is a property of the *set*, it is the direct consequence of the
frozen 2/2/2 composition applied to a repository whose T-1/T-2 are L1, and it
should be stated in the report rather than discovered by a reader. It also means
the pilot's headline claim must be phrased over the per-level breakdown, never
over "the six questions" as a whole.

## 5. Cross-repository comparability

### 5.1 What is comparable, and what the frozen protocol fixes

The two sets are comparable in exactly the ways the protocol defines, and no
wider (CONFIRMED by reading both documents):

- **Level composition is identical**: 2 L1, 2 L2, 2 L3 in each set — attrs Q-A/Q-C
  L1, Q-E/Q-F L2, Q-B/Q-D L3; tqdm T-1/T-2 L1, T-3/T-4 L2, T-5/T-6 L3.
- **The primary metric is unchanged and stays unblended**: the proportion of
  score-2 answers, per question; time reported separately; calibration
  (confidence − correctness) separately; per-level breakdown (experiment doc
  §7.1–§7.3). **This review does not compute, and does not permit, a single
  merged "tool effect" number over the two repositories**: the two sets are
  different questions about different code, so a repository difference and a
  question-set difference are the same measurement. Results are reported per
  repository and per question (CONFIRMED as the rule in both documents; INFERRED
  as necessary because no other defensible decomposition exists).
- **The arms, cap, orientation and decision rule are identical**, and `explain`
  is excluded on both.

### 5.2 What differs, and how it could bend a conclusion

| Difference | Direction it could push | How the design handles it | Marker |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Documentation density.** attrs maintains a changelog (72.9 KB) and its L1 sites are reachable from it; tqdm has **no changelog in the tree** (the only name-matched hit is a CI workflow) | tqdm's set should be *harder*, so a tqdm-set improvement is stronger evidence; but its L2/L3 answers live in commit messages, which tqdm writes at the highest measured explanatory rate (15%) — so "harder" is not uniform | Selection was made on this property on purpose; each question's calibration record states what the README does and does not say | CONFIRMED |
| **Subject matter and prior knowledge.** attrs: class construction, exception semantics, interpreter versions. tqdm: terminals, ANSI, processes, rate arithmetic | A participant who has *used* tqdm has real advantages on T-1/T-2 (visible bar behaviour) and some on T-5/T-6 (multiprocessing, progress-bar UX); attrs' questions favour people read in Python internals | The counterbalanced crossover puts a given participant's tqdm session in only one arm, so across 6–8 participants the confound is *spread* rather than controlled; record per-repository prior familiarity per participant and report it (the document already requires this) | INFERRED |
| **Question spread.** Four of attrs' six are in one file by one maintainer; tqdm's span four subsystems by different authors | More within-set heterogeneity in tqdm, so a single "tqdm effect" is less meaningful than a single "attrs effect" already was | Report per question, per level; do not average | CONFIRMED |
| **History shape.** attrs squashed PR commits; tqdm merge-centred releases | The tool's `commit` view may be less useful on a merge commit; a participant may land on a release bump | Listed as unresolved in the document (§9.2); the derivation never needed a merge commit, and the questions name their own commits | INFERRED / partly UNKNOWN |
| **History length and size.** 11.7 vs 12.9 years; 1,845 vs 2,132 commits | Small; both far above the floors | — | CONFIRMED |
| **The one-repository fallback.** §6.2 allows splitting one set in half and counterbalancing | Then repository difficulty and tool effect are confounded, and the learning effect inside one repository resurfaces | The document states this three times (§6, §7, §9.1); running both repositories is the design's intent and this review's recommendation | CONFIRMED |

**What the crossover does and does not control.** It controls the order of arms
and the pair of repositories (a repository effect shows up as a repository
effect). It does **not** control prior familiarity with a specific repository,
and it does not control question-level difficulty — the two repositories'
questions are different instruments, so "attrs vs tqdm" must never be read as
"easy vs hard". With n≈6, a per-repository effect size is not estimable at all
(each participant contributes three questions per repository); the experiment
document already forbids p-values and per-repository claims, and this review
re-states it as a disclosure item.

## 6. Second-scorer readiness, and the minimal supplement

**What already exists (CONFIRMED by reading both documents).** The rubric (§7.2),
per-question anchors in four forms each — correct / partially correct / correctly
uncertain / wrong — with the SHAs and the commands that reproduce each ground
truth; the requirement that every question carry a written correctly-uncertain
form; and the threat disclosed in §9: the tool's author prepared the ground truth
and would be the scorer, "if none is available, say so in the report".

**What is missing for an independent blind scoring pass (CONFIRMED by reading
Appendix C and the second-repository document's §8).**

1. **The answer sheet carries the arm.** Appendix C prints `Arm: git / tool` at
   the top of every sheet.
2. **The "How you found it" field reveals the arm**: a treatment participant
   will write `archaeology structure …` or a command name.
3. **No disagreement rule**: the protocol says "report it", not what counts.
4. **No agreement record**: nothing says what to record, or that kappa is
   meaningless at n≈6.
5. **The U rule lives in the rubric**, and each question's uncertain form lives
   in its own entry — but no single scorer-facing page assembles them, so a
   scorer working question by question can mis-apply U.
6. **Nothing checks whether blinding worked** — "prevent the scorer guessing the
   arm" is an intention, not a measurement.

**The minimal supplement** (a specification; nothing below has been done — no
scorer exists):

1. **Transcription, then blinding.** After each session, transcribe the paper
   sheet into one text file per participant per question. The blind copy
   contains exactly: participant code, question id, the answer text, the
   evidence named, minutes, confidence. It does **not** contain the arm, the
   participant's name, the repository, or the "how you found it" field. The
   facilitator keeps the mapping (code → participant, arm, commands used) in a
   separate file that never reaches the scorer.
2. **Arm-leak normalisation.** In the blind copy, replace tool-specific strings
   in the answer or evidence field with a neutral token: the command names
   (`analyze`, `ast`, `timeline`, `hotspots`, `files`, `file`, `commit`,
   `structure`, `cochange`), `archaeology …`, and `--history` → `[a command]`.
   Commit SHAs, file paths, line numbers and versions stay verbatim: both arms
   can cite them, and they are the evidence the rubric scores.
3. **One scorer page per question** (two sets, twelve pages), assembled from the
   documents as they stand — no rewriting: the four scoring forms, the evidence
   that counts, the date-acceptance note, and the UNKNOWN sub-claims. The scorer
   gets both sets' ground truths and the rubric, and nothing else.
4. **U versus wrong uncertainty, written as a rule the scorer applies:** score
   `U` = 2 **only** where that question's "correctly uncertain" line says
   uncertainty is the truth; where the ground truth exists and the answer is "the
   history does not say", score 0. Per question: T-1 **n/a**; T-2 **n/a**; T-3
   applies to the *why `__new__` rather than `__init__`* sub-claim; T-4 applies
   to the *post-division rather than initialising at the first value*
   sub-claim; T-5 applies to the *physical mechanism of the leak* sub-claim; T-6
   applies to the *author date vs commit date* sub-claim. (The attrs set's
   entries carry their own equivalents.)
5. **Disagreement rule, fixed before scoring.** Two scorers score independently,
   from the blind copy alone. Per answer they record two scores. Where they
   differ, both re-read the ground truth and the answer together and record a
   single resolved score **with the reason**; if they still differ, the answer
   is recorded as contested, and for the primary endpoint it takes the **lower**
   of the two scores. Contested answers are listed in the report.
6. **Agreement record.** Report: how many answers were double-scored; the raw
   agreement rate; the contested list with reasons; and the statement that a
   chance-corrected statistic (kappa) is not interpretable at this size and is
   therefore not computed. If only one scorer exists, the report must say so
   plainly — "single scorer, the tool's author" — as §9 of the frozen document
   requires.
7. **Blinding check.** If the scorer is willing, have them guess the arm for each
   answer after scoring, and report the guess rate against chance. This converts
   "we blinded it" into a number, and it is the only honest way to claim the
   blinding held.
8. **Score from the written answer alone** — no re-prompting, no facilitator
   context, no session narration (there is none).

**Marker summary:** items 1–2 are needed only if a second scorer materialises;
their absence does not affect the primary endpoint's *collection* — it affects
its *independence*, which the frozen protocol already discloses as a threat.
**UNKNOWN:** whether any second scorer will exist.

## 7. The Memory sub-experiment: disposition

**What the documents require (CONFIRMED).** The frozen protocol's session
timeline puts the Memory sub-experiment inside the 90-minute session at minutes
70–80 (§6.4), with a protocol of its own (§8): one piece of knowledge, two
conditions, measure whether it was found and how long it took; the knowledge
must be **true and non-derivable from the tool** — in practice restated from the
repository's own documentation; and the *retention* claim (knowledge surviving
months) cannot be measured by this design at all. The attrs set has one verified
candidate (`docs/init.md:506`, the `cache_hash` warning) and a rule that keeps
the six questions away from its island (§8, experiment doc §4.1 step 7). The
second-repository document states the tqdm materials were **not started** (§9.7),
"and §8 of the experiment document requires a *true, non-derivable* statement;
the tqdm README/`docs` would have to be searched for one the same way".

**Answering the three questions the task poses:**

1. **Is it a necessary part of this pilot?** **No (CONFIRMED).** The pilot's
   primary endpoint is the proportion of score-2 answers on the six questions
   (§7.1); the Memory block contributes no answer to any of them, is measured by
   a different quantity (time-to-find at all), and appears in no decision rule
   (§10's four outcomes are computed from the six questions only). Its presence
   in the session timeline is a scheduling choice, not a dependency.
2. **Can it be a separate experiment?** **Yes (CONFIRMED by reading §8).** It
   has its own claim, its own conditions and its own measurement, and §8 itself
   says the better version is a different study (inside a team, on a repository
   the team owns). Nothing in it needs the six questions to have been run.
3. **Is it ready?** **No (CONFIRMED).** For tqdm there is no candidate memory at
   all, and inventing one is forbidden by §8's own constraint and by this task's
   rules — a fabricated memory would measure "does a made-up note help". For
   attrs there is one candidate, which is enough for a study of one repository,
   not for a two-repository pilot. Also unresolved: the block runs *after* the
   six questions for every participant, so its two conditions are not the arms,
   and an unattended improvisation in the last ten minutes of a session would
   contaminate the debrief.

**Decision (the user, 2026-10-09): the sub-experiment is separated, and the pilot
is not delayed for it.** Concretely:

- The pilot runs the six questions, the briefing, the five-minute orientation
  and the debrief; the §6.4 Memory block is **deferred to a separate experiment**,
  on §7's evidence. This is a scope reduction of the session, not a change to the
  primary metric, the rubric, the thresholds or the decision rule — none of which
  involve Memory. **The deferral is recorded where a facilitator would meet it:**
  §6.4's session table and Appendix B's script in
  `docs/v0.5-understanding-experiment.md` now say the block is not run, §8's head
  carries the reasons, §9.7 of the second-repository document says the missing
  tqdm material is no longer a startup condition, and PROGRESS.md records the
  decision with its reasons. The frozen document's hash changed for this
  annotation only (§1, §10 item 9).
- The Memory study then starts from §8 as it stands, with its own materials:
  search each repository's own docs for a genuine statement the tool cannot
  derive (attrs' candidate is already verified; tqdm's is not searched), and keep
  the island rule in force for whatever question set accompanies it.
- **No memory is invented here.** The review's only contribution to the future
  study is the disposition and the constraint that the statement must already
  exist in the repository.

## 10. The startup verdict

**READY WITH DISCLOSED LIMITATIONS.**

**Why not READY.** The material set is complete and verified, but four things
about it are true and must be said in the experiment report rather than
discovered by its readers: the L1 pair is expected to saturate; only two of the
six questions discriminate; the two repositories' sets are different instruments
and their per-repository numbers must not be merged or read as difficulty; and
the scorer may be the tool's author, which the frozen protocol already names as
a threat.

**Why not NOT READY.** No unresolved issue affects the six questions' fairness
or the scoreability of a written answer: every non-L1 answer is reachable by the
control with `git blame`/`-S`/`--grep` alone (re-verified, §3.1); every answer is
supported by at least two independent artifacts and has four written scoring
forms; every L3 site's event list contains the commits the answer needs and no
blind spots (re-verified, §3.2–§3.4); and the one conditionally blocking item
(the Memory block) has a recommended resolution that is a recorded scope
decision, not missing evidence (§7, §8 item 3).

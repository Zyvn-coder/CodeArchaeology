# v0.4 explanation schema

This is Unit 3 of v0.4: the shape the answer comes back in. The problem
definition (`docs/v0.4-problem-definition.md`) fixes the question, the evidence
contract (`docs/v0.4-evidence-contract.md`) fixes what may be used to answer it,
and this fixes the form of the answer.

## 1. Why the shape is frozen rather than requested

The obvious design — ask the model for an explanation and print what it says —
fails for a reason that has nothing to do with model quality. A paragraph of
prose has no seam in it. "The author added `refresh_token()` to fix login expiry"
reads as one finding, and its first half is in the data and its second half is
not. A reader cannot tell where the evidence stopped, and neither can a test.

The user's rule for this unit:

> 不要让模型直接返回一大段自由文本。先冻结结构。

So the model does not return prose. It returns a structure, the structure
separates the three kinds of statement, and the tool decides how each one is
printed. **A model that wants to blur evidence and interpretation has nowhere to
put the result.**

**The status of the whole answer is the contract in
`docs/v0.4-problem-definition.md` §12**: what the tool derives is a fact with a
test behind it, and what a model writes from those facts is a reading of them,
never a record of what happened. What this schema does about it is make the
distinction structural rather than a disclaimer — the seam is in the headings, so
a sentence cannot choose which side of it to sit on.

## 2. The three words

The output distinguishes three kinds of statement, and they are the evidence
contract's three kinds under the names this layer uses:

| Here | In the evidence contract | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Observed** | Fact | What the bundle held. Checkable against a stored row. |
| **Possible** | Inference | A reading of what was observed. Offered, marked, and it names what it rests on. |
| **Unknown** | Unknown | Outside the data. Named so the reader can see the edge, never filled in. |

The words differ on purpose, and the difference is not decoration. *Fact* claims
something about the world; *Observed* claims only that the tool saw it in the
data, which is all the tool can honestly say. The mapping is stated here so the
two documents cannot drift into two vocabularies for one idea.

## 3. The schema

```
Explanation
├── summary              prose; observed-only, and it cites what it restates
├── observed_changes[]   one entry per thing that happened
│     ├── id             stable within one explanation, e.g. "oc1"
│     ├── statement      one sentence, in the file's own terms
│     └── evidence[]     pointers into the bundle
├── evidence[]           the citations themselves, each naming a stored row
├── possible_reasons[]   candidates
│     ├── statement      one sentence, marked as a candidate by its field
│     ├── based_on[]     the observed-change ids it rests on — never empty
│     └── confidence     derived by the tool, never returned by the model — §8
└── uncertainty[]        what could not be determined, and which absence it is
```

The six top-level fields are the user's list. What this unit adds is the inside
of two of them — the ids, and `based_on` — because without them the separation is
a formatting convention rather than something a program can check.

## 4. The fields, and their rules

### `summary`

Prose, and the most dangerous field in the schema, because it is the one place
free text is allowed.

**Observed only.** The summary may restate what is in `observed_changes` and
nothing else. It may not introduce a reason, a motive, a judgement or a
prediction, and it may not carry a candidate reason even in hedged form — that is
what `possible_reasons` is for, and a hedged claim in the summary is the exact
failure the user's example describes.

**It cites what it restates.** Every sentence names the `observed_changes` ids it
draws on. A summary sentence with no id is a sentence with nothing behind it.

### `observed_changes[]`

One entry per thing that happened, each a single sentence in the file's own
terms: "`auth.py` gained `refresh_token`", "`auth.py` was renamed from
`login.py`", "the file did not parse at this commit".

**Every entry carries at least one evidence pointer.** An observed change with no
pointer is a claim the bundle does not support, and it does not get printed.

### `evidence[]`

The citations, each naming something that exists: a commit sha, a path, a
definition's kind and qualified name, a row in `commit_files`, a parse failure,
or a statistic with its denominator. This is the same list the evidence contract
§3 enumerates, and nothing may appear here that is not on it.

Evidence is a separate field from `observed_changes` for one reason: a claim and
its citation are two different things, and a reader checking the answer wants the
second without having to parse it out of the first.

### `possible_reasons[]`

The candidates. Three rules, all of them from the evidence contract:

1. **A candidate is a candidate.** It is in this field and not in `summary`, and
   the rendering marks it as a possibility rather than a finding.
2. **It names what it rests on.** `based_on` is never empty. A reason with
   nothing behind it is not offered.
3. **It does not state intent.** "This is consistent with a fix for a failing
   refresh" is a candidate. "The author fixed a failing refresh" is not a
   candidate at all — it is the *Unknown* of §2, and there is no field for it.

The field may be empty. A commit whose evidence supports no candidate gets an
empty list, and that is a correct answer.

### `uncertainty[]`

What the tool could not determine, and which of the evidence contract's four
absences it is:

- a definition is gone — absence is the evidence;
- a version did not parse — **nothing could be read**;
- a path has no version at this commit — the file was not part of it;
- a merge — git reports no diff for a merge.

This field is not a disclaimer and it is not padding. It is where the answer says
where the data stops, and it is the field a reader should look at first when the
`possible_reasons` list is empty.

## 5. The user's two sentences, taken apart

The pair the user wrote is the whole unit in two lines.

> **Observed**: `auth.py` 新增了 `refresh_token()`。
>
> **Possible reason**: 这可能与同一 commit 中新增的 token 生命周期处理有关。

Both halves are supported. The first names something the bundle holds — a
definition created at this commit. The second is marked as a candidate by its own
field, and it rests on something the bundle holds too: another change in the same
commit. It says *可能*, and the structure says the same thing.

> **作者为了修复登录过期问题，所以新增了 refresh_token。**

This one is wrong three times over, and each is a different rule:

| The defect | The rule it breaks |
|---|---|
| "为了" — a purpose, stated as fact | Intent is *Unknown*. There is no field for it, in any phrasing. |
| "所以" — a cause, asserted between two events | A cause is a reading, and this one is not marked as a candidate. |
| "登录过期问题" — the defect | It is outside the repository. The bundle does not hold it, so the answer may not either. |

Note that the second sentence is not wrong because it is *unlikely*. It may well
be true. It is wrong because the tool cannot know it, and the schema exists to
make that visible instead of making it sound settled.

## 6. What the validator can check, and what it cannot

The schema is worth freezing only if something holds the model to it. It does:
`validation.py` is a unit of its own, and it runs three passes in the order the
pipeline names them — the text is a JSON object, it has the shape asked for and
*only* that shape, and every citation in it names something the bundle holds.
Anything that fails a pass is refused whole, and the refusal names the check.

**Checkable:**

- Every `observed_changes` entry has at least one evidence pointer, and the
  pointer names something in the bundle — checked against the set the context
  actually holds, so a path used as a commit is refused rather than accepted
  because both strings are known.
- Every `possible_reasons` entry has a non-empty `based_on`, and every id in it
  names an `observed_changes` entry.
- Every `summary` sentence cites ids that exist, and the summary cites something
  at all.
- No `possible_reasons` entry appears in `summary`.
- The absences in `uncertainty` are drawn from the known kinds.
- **The answer has no field the schema does not have.** A field nobody reads is a
  claim nobody verified, and the answer that is shown has to be the answer that
  was checked. This is what refuses a `confidence` the model sends: the tool
  derives it from `based_on`, so a model reporting its own is answering a
  question it was not asked, and the refusal says so rather than dropping the
  field quietly.

**Not checkable:**

- Whether an observed change is *true* of the repository — the check is that it
  is in the bundle, not that the bundle is right. That is `analyze`'s and `ast`'s
  contract, held by their own tests.
- Whether a summary sentence stays inside the fact it cites. A sentence can cite
  `oc1` and still overstate it, and no program will catch that.

The second list is written down because trap 34's lesson applies here exactly:
**when a document is checked mechanically, ask what the check cannot see**, and
say so in the same document. A reader who knows the summary is only partly
checked reads it differently from one who assumes it is validated.

Every rule above has a case in `tests/test_validation.py`, one row per way an
answer can be wrong. The table is exhaustive on purpose: a rule with no case is a
rule that could be deleted without anything going red, and the messages are
checked as well as the refusal, because "rejected" with no reason is what a user
cannot act on.

## 7. What an empty field means

- `possible_reasons: []` — the evidence supports no candidate. Correct, and the
  `uncertainty` list should explain why.
- `uncertainty: []` — nothing was missing. It is not a claim that the answer is
  complete, only that no absence was recorded.
- `summary` — never empty. If there is nothing to summarise, there is nothing to
  explain, and the command should say that rather than return a structure.

## 8. `confidence` is derived, never reported

**The user's ruling: option B, per reason.** The field stays, and what it holds is
worked out from the bundle rather than asked of the model.

**The conflict it was decided against.** A confidence value produced by the model
is the model's own assessment of its answer. It is not in the bundle, it is not
derivable from the bundle, and it is not the same number twice. Evidence First
requires every conclusion to be traceable to a commit, a diff or an AST node, and
a self-reported confidence is traceable to nothing. Worse, a number *reads* as a
fact — "0.8" would be the most authoritative-looking thing the answer contains,
and the least supported.

**What the field holds.** How many observed changes the candidate rests on, and
the commits those changes came from: "this candidate rests on 3 observed changes,
all in this commit." Nothing else, and no score.

**The model does not emit it.** `confidence` is filled in by the tool from
`based_on`, which is what makes it traceable: the count can be read straight off
the answer, and the answer can be checked against the bundle. A field the model
cannot write is a field the model cannot inflate. **And it is refused if it
arrives anyway** — a `confidence` in the model's output fails the schema pass
with a message saying the model does not write it, rather than being dropped
quietly, because a dropped field would mean the answer shown is not the answer
that was checked.

**It measures how much evidence there is, not how good the reason is**, and the
rendering has to say so in as many words. The project already has this shape —
`files` ends by saying what a deleted row means, because a list of them invites
exactly one misreading — and this is the same kind of sentence. A candidate
resting on three facts is not therefore a better reason than one resting on two;
it is a candidate with more of the evidence behind it, which is a different claim.

**Per reason, not one number for the answer.** The user's list put `confidence` at
the top level, where it can only rate the whole answer — which averages a
well-supported candidate together with a weak one and produces a number that
describes neither.

**The two options not taken**, with their reasons, so a later unit argues with a
reason rather than re-adding one quietly:

- **Drop the field.** The strength of a candidate is already visible from what
  `based_on` holds. Rejected because a derived count is cheaper to read than a
  list to count, and it costs nothing in honesty once the tool computes it.
- **Keep it as the model's self-report.** Rejected because it stays untraceable
  however it is labelled: Evidence First is not satisfied by printing a warning
  above a number that should not exist.

## 9. What this unit does not decide

- **The rendering.** The schema is the answer's structure; how the terminal draws
  it — headings, colour, order — is not fixed here.
- **Whether `--json` emits this structure directly.** It is the natural reading of
  the project's own convention, but it is an interface decision.
- **How the model is asked for this.** Whether the schema is enforced by a prompt,
  by a constrained decoding mode, or by a parse-and-retry loop is the freeze's.
- **What happens to an answer that fails validation** — refused, retried, or
  printed with the failure named.

Everything Unit 1 §10 and the evidence contract §10 left open stays open: the
provider, the no-AI path, the CI story, the storage question, the diff's bound,
and whether the first version ships the diff at all.
